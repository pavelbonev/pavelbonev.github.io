Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026 · Applies to the NEARena app for Android and iPhone (com.simplia.nearena)

In short: We have no servers of our own and keep no data about you. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Playing over the internet is optional: only then does the app use Epic Online Services to connect the phones. Epic then gets a random ID, your IP address and the room code: nothing that names you, but under EU law (the GDPR) the ID and the IP address still count as personal data.

Who is responsible

NEARena is made by Pavel Bonev, a private individual in Bulgaria. He is the controller of the personal data described in this policy (“we”). Contact: simplia.apps@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

These things are kept only on your phone, in the app's private storage. We never receive them:

  • your nickname and a random player ID the app makes the first time it starts;
  • the people you have played with, your favourites and your wins against them;
  • your settings (bot level, board options and the like).

If Android backup is turned on, Android may keep a copy of this data in your Google account, so that it comes back on a new phone. You decide that in your phone's settings. Uninstalling the app deletes the data from the phone.

On iPhone, if iCloud Backup is on, iOS keeps a copy of this data in your iCloud, so that it comes back on a new iPhone. You decide that in the iPhone's settings. Deleting the app deletes the data from the iPhone.

Bluetooth

  • Bluetooth is used only to connect the phones in a room, directly to each other.
  • While you host a room, phones nearby that look for rooms can see your nickname and a short code made from your player ID, so that friends recognise you before they join. An iPhone that hosts a room shows only the nickname, without that code.
  • The phones in a room exchange the nicknames, player IDs and moves of the game. Nothing else is sent, and nothing leaves the room.
  • On Android 8 to 11 the system shows a location permission, because Android needs it to search for Bluetooth devices. NEARena does not use, read or store your location. On Android 12 and later the app does not ask for location at all.
  • On iPhone, iOS asks once whether NEARena may use Bluetooth. The app does not ask for your location and does not use it.

Playing over the internet (optional)

A room plays over Bluetooth unless its host invites friends over the internet with a room code. Only then, and only on the phones that take part, does the app connect to Epic Online Services, a service of Epic Games that finds the room by its code and connects the phones. We use it instead of servers of our own. If you never play over the internet, the app does not connect to Epic at all.

What Epic receives:

  • a random ID for the app on this phone (Epic's “Device ID” login). There is no Epic account: Epic gets no name, e-mail or phone number;
  • your IP address, which every connection over the internet needs;
  • the room code and the random IDs of the phones in the room, while the room is open. No nicknames are stored with them, and they disappear when the room closes;
  • when you invite over the internet, a code made from the random player ID of each of your favourites and from yours (a hash: not the ID itself, and no nickname), so that your favourites find the room without a code. It disappears with the room;
  • technical data and events that Epic's library in the app sends together with the random ID: its version and the version of the phone's system (Android or iOS), which of its functions the app used, how long its requests took, errors, and when a room was created, connected or closed. Epic uses them for statistics about its service.

The only other time the app connects to Epic is when you open “Join a room” and have favourites you have already played with over the internet: while that screen is open, it looks for rooms with your code every few seconds, so that your favourites' rooms show on the radar without a room code. Without such favourites, that screen looks only over Bluetooth.

The phones in the room then exchange the same things as over Bluetooth (nicknames, player IDs and moves), encrypted. Usually they connect directly, and then each phone in the room can see the IP addresses of the others, as in any direct connection on the internet. When a network does not allow a direct connection, the data goes through Epic's relay servers, which only pass it on.

Why and on what basis. This data is used only to connect the phones for the game you asked to play over the internet, so the legal basis is providing that service to you (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). Epic processes it for us, as our processor, only to run the service. The technical data and events from its library Epic also uses for statistics about its own service; for that part Epic is responsible itself, under the Epic Games Privacy Policy.

Where. Epic's servers are in the United States and other countries. Epic is not part of the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, so the transfer relies on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in a data processing agreement, which we asked Epic to sign in October 2026. Until then, Epic's agreement with us already binds it to use the data only to run the service.

How long. The room code, the IDs of the phones in it and the favourites' codes disappear when the room closes. Epic keeps the random ID while the app on your phone uses it, and its logs and statistics, which include the IP address, for as long as it needs them to run and secure the service. We keep none of it.

Purchases

NEARena sells nothing: everything in it is free, and the app does not ask Google Play or the App Store about purchases.

Older versions for Android sold NEARena Pro through Google Play. If you bought it, Google handled the payment under the Google Privacy Policy and keeps your purchase history; we never saw your name, e-mail or payment details.

Your rights and deleting your data

On Android

  • On your phone: Settings → Apps → NEARena → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the app. This deletes everything the app keeps.
  • The copy in Android backup: in the backup settings of your Google account (on most phones Settings → Google → Backup).
  • The ID at Epic: clearing the storage or uninstalling the app deletes its key from the phone, and after that the ID can no longer be linked to your phone. The next time you play over the internet, the app gets a new one.
  • Your purchase history: Google keeps it; you can see and manage it at myaccount.google.com.

On iPhone

  • On your iPhone: delete the app (touch and hold its icon, then Remove App → Delete App). This deletes everything the app keeps.
  • The copy in iCloud Backup: Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Backups → this iPhone, where you can switch NEARena off (this deletes its copy).
  • The ID at Epic: iOS may keep its key in the iPhone's keychain even after the app is deleted, but NEARena installed again deletes that key before it first connects to Epic and gets a new ID. After that the old ID can no longer be linked to your iPhone.

We ourselves hold no data about you, so there is nothing for us to delete. If you write to us, we will confirm that.

Under the GDPR you have the right to ask for access to your data, its correction or deletion, a copy of it, or to restrict or object to its use. We keep no data, and the ID at Epic is random, so we usually cannot tell which data at Epic is yours (Article 11 GDPR); the quickest way to delete it is to clear the app's data as above, which ends its link with your phone. Write to us and we will help with anything else. You can also complain to a data protection authority: in Bulgaria the Commission for Personal Data Protection (cpdp.bg), or the authority in your own country.

Children

NEARena asks no one, children included, for a name, e-mail, photo or location. There are no ads, no chat and no way to find strangers: you play only with phones in the same room or with friends who have your room code or are among your favourites. Playing over the internet works the same for everyone, as described above.
